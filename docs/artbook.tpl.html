The Drawn
Cast

Every character in CLAWBYTE / NOSTOS — and the world they stand in — redrawn by hand in one style: a fine dark contour that breaks — thickening on the shadow side, tapering to nothing on the lit side — over three or four flat steps of value, matte, with a dry-brush grain.

  • 22 hero cells
  • 64 cast plates
  • 6 guardians
  • 131 rig parts
  • 21 world plates
Every plate is real art. Each one is a generated drawing, cut out and placed into the sheet the game already loads — no mock-ups.
Amber marks a wind-up. #ffc24a is the game's reserved telegraph colour. A plate carrying it is a tell the player must be able to read.
The dark tile is the stage. These are cutouts drawn for a dark room; they are shown on the ground they actually stand on.

What this pass had to solve

The field cannot be won by prompting

Told “flat black, no ground”, the generator paints an ink wash; told louder, it paints a white paper border with a brushed black rectangle inside it. The background is removed mechanically instead, by a keyer that walks inward from the frame and stops at drawn lines.

A broken contour is a doorway

The style's whole idea — a line that stops — is also a hole a flood fill walks through, and the first cuts came back as outlines with hollow torsos. The keyer restores anything enclosed on all eight sides, because the colour was always there; only the transparency was wrong.

A guardian is a rig, not a picture

Six of these characters are addressed by absolute pixel rectangle. A head that returns four pixels taller is not scaled — it is drawn from the same rectangle, and the animal comes apart at the neck. Every restyled part is fitted back onto the box the original held and clipped to its outline, so the rig cannot move.

Naming a thing forbids nothing

“No brick courses” returned six brick walls; “the lower third is empty” returned a sheet of white paper. Saying what the picture IS — irregular lumps packed like scree, the bottom half is solid black paint — got both right on the first try.

The warning colour is a system, not a palette

A style pass that says “desaturate everything” silently deletes how the game tells you what is coming. Every wind-up plate carries its amber as an explicit exception to its own colour rule — and a test measures that each tell still raises the amber above that creature's own resting state.
