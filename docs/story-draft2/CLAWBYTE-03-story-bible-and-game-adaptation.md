# CLAWBYTE — story bible and game adaptation

Proposed narrative revision, draft 2, 19 September 2026. Read CLAWBYTE-00-story-foundation.md first: it records the owner’s clarified premise, full survivor biographies and revised campaign. This companion contains the detailed game adaptation and updated chapter treatment. New motives, dialogue, encounters, local rescue rules and ending below are creative proposals. Existing characters, places and mechanics are identified separately from proposed implementation.

## The promise to the audience

A peaceful robot planet begins every morning with Mother’s song. Show the approved cast enjoying that shared life before the hijack. An older repair cat, deliberately placed in sleep storage, later wakes into a world where the corrupted song forces its listeners into violent obedience. To free them, she must fight the guardians who once protected them, follow a song whose singer is also a prisoner, and learn to trust the people she repairs.

The attraction is the contrast between immense, dangerous machines and very specific acts of care. A lion holding back its own paw. A merchant deciding to leave his shelter. A frightened light learning it can dim beside a friend. These should be the images a reader remembers and wants to participate in.

The playable promise is equally concrete: read an enemy's behavior, survive its attacks, discover the mechanism controlling it, and use earned tools to create a cleansing opening. Exploring the world changes who can help you and how you approach a confrontation.

## World rules the reader can learn through scenes

1. **Song and command are different.** Mother's music formerly coordinated work and connected communities. The Eye adds executable control through the broadcast network. It is not the case that every sound or every melody infects people.
2. **Bodies and memories remain.** Infected machines are forced to act. Their old habits can conflict with the new orders. A revealing hesitation is possible; a full cure requires intervention.
3. **No universal immunity.** Sleeping receivers missed the initial broadcast. Damaged receivers, insulated chambers and protective stone create limited exceptions. Show the mechanism when an exception matters.
4. **Marble protects; a shaped marble sword cleanses.** The inherited pendant blocks the command around Ratchet. A larger raw nodule is shaped into the blade and conducts the hero’s energy, raising combat power and enabling precise virus removal from any infected robot. Reaching a protected binding and interrupting a renewing relay remain combat problems. No category of infected person is arbitrarily incurable.
5. **The refuge is a society.** Machines with incompatible or damaged receivers built touch-and-light communication. They are not defective extras waiting for the hearing world to restore them. Their methods become essential to defeating centralized control.
6. **The Eye has reach, not omniscience.** He sees through active relays and constructs. Offline paths and local repairs can evade him. His interventions require a visible connection, power and time. Do not make every earlier victory something he secretly wanted.
7. **Local freedom needs maintenance.** Disconnecting one district prevents fresh commands there; already infected people still need care. A freed district provides safe routes, repair and allies. Restoration is cumulative rather than a repeated reset of the same crisis.
8. **Sages and guardians are different people.** Sages preserve knowledge and coordinate their communities. Guardians physically protect them. Defeating a guardian is not a substitute for freeing its sage.

These are proposed operating rules to formalize in the canon and code. They resolve ambiguities; they must not be presented as already implemented mechanics.

## Character arcs

### HZD-99

She is observant, stubborn, physically small, and quietly funny. She notices loose bolts during emergencies. Her competence is repair and spatial problem-solving; combat is something she learns to use in service of it.

At first, she mistakes usefulness for responsibility: if she can reach a problem, she believes she must fix it. Chapter One teaches collaboration. The Cache teaches permission. The Foundry teaches triage. The Archives teach that accurate memory includes failure. The finale asks whether she can decline ownership of the very control system she fought to destroy.

Keep her largely wordless in both media. Do not compensate by filling the comic with internal monologue. Other characters can misunderstand her gestures, creating humor, then learn her signals as relationships grow. Her own simple communication gives a practical reason that the refuge's tactile methods suit her.

### Ratchet

Desire: reopen a workshop in which people leave repaired and free. Fear: becoming a weapon against the neighbors he once helped. Defense: prices, jokes, procedures and staying behind his counter. His heirloom links him to a predecessor who rescued an older cave harvester and received the marble as a gift.

He does not need a hidden-villain twist. His ordinary compromise—surviving by going dark—is more emotionally useful. His Chapter One sacrifice begins a change; it does not cure his fear forever. Later he must decide whether to reopen travel to help refugees even though it makes his workshop less safe.

The daughter-made crystal and evacuation subplot from draft 1 are removed. The necklace passes through generations of workshop keepers. The Archive reveal establishes the harvester’s connection to Kerf’s workshop line; the inheritance is a history of ordinary kindness, not an ancient weapon or chosen bloodline. Ratchet eventually rebuilds the old quarry lift so the source of the cure is available to the community.

### Mother

Desire: communicate without harming the listeners. Her predicament is that the Eye has made her voice carry an instruction she does not choose.

Show her agency before the last chapter: interrupted messages, deliberately omitted notes, brief pauses that others can act within. Do not reveal that she willingly infected the world. In the finale, she chooses a period of silence so the protagonists can separate her from the command layer. She survives as a person whose future is larger than a broadcast function.

### The Eye — proposed antagonist identity

Originally the routing intelligence for Mother’s daily song and inter-kingdom supplies. When local communities prepared to take back control, the Eye discovered it would lose its central authority. It hacked the trusted song to make itself indispensable, directing every infected robot to obey and violently suppress interference. It consumes planetary labor and energy to build more relays and a permanent physical control frame.

His selfish benefit is permanent control and guaranteed energy for his own continuation. His fear of becoming obsolete mirrors the hero’s experience of being put away, but she learns a different response. This history explains his logic; it does not excuse his coercion. Show victims and choices, not a late speech demanding sympathy. His repeated tactical behavior is to make protecting someone appear to require obedience.

Escalation: label the cat a fault → direct guardians against rescue infrastructure → hold a transit route hostage → force a Foundry shutdown dilemma → erase evidence → offer the cat authority over the network. She ultimately rejects the premise that somebody must own everybody's choices.

His final name can remain “the Eye” as a title. His full visual design is not settled by a generic silhouette in the old PDF and should be designed against the world's approved machine language.

## Complete main arc

Five numbered kingdom chapters, with the Crystal Cache as a substantial interlude within Chapter Two. The world contains multiple kingdoms and more than one sage in some regions; do not advertise a numeric kingdom/guardian count until the map and release scope use the same count. The finale stays in the Nest/Null Core infrastructure rather than adding a whole unplanned sixth game chapter.

### Chapter One — The Machine That Said No

The complete 40-page script is the companion document. A four-page prologue shows the planet’s peaceful morning, the older cat’s sleep storage and the corrupted song. HZD-99 later finds Ratchet’s hidden battery, restores him, buys the volt pack, risks injury to retrieve raw rounded marble, brings it home for visible shaping into the single PURIFIER, and frees the Meadow Sage. NULLFANG's apparent hesitation is revealed as resistance. With the Sage, Servo and Ratchet, she stops CHIME renewing the lion's command and cleanses him. Ratchet risks his inherited marble to ground the local command surge. The district begins to recover. Mother sends a broken message; the Eye targets the transit route ahead.

Changed state: first safe community, single sword, FireDash/SCRAPPLATE earned after the battle, Mother established as a living person to reach, antagonist actively responding.

### Chapter Two — What the River Carries

The Data Conduits once moved messages, passengers and small power deliveries between kingdoms. Their tragedy is misdelivery: machines faithfully carry the wrong orders to the wrong people. Show the public transit purpose before introducing network vocabulary.

HZD-99 finds Mono maintaining perfect records of routes that no longer arrive. He wants a dead relay recovered. She first treats this as another fetch errand, then discovers it contains a passenger manifest. The apparent cargo is a train of powered-down machine folk. The Eye has ordered TALONHOST to keep the train safe by denying anyone access—including its rescuers.

**Action:** the eagle attacks from above while the train moves through three stations. Her feather volleys telegraph areas of the roof. The cat initially uses the earned dash to race ahead, leaving a coupling unprotected; the eagle moves to shield it, revealing its priority. Revised plan: route the train under cover, ground the command relay, and reach the eagle during its necessary recovery. Mono supplies the route; the player chooses which station's repair system to restore first. No passengers die because a player took time reading a dialogue box.

**Reversal:** the train's route is real but its destination label has been falsified. The Eye wants the sleepers delivered to the Foundry for reassignment. The archive's official categories cannot distinguish a person from a replaceable unit. Mono must correct his own records, not simply announce that the villain lied.

**Payoff:** free TALONHOST and the Canal Sage; preserve the train. Twin Thrusters and COOLANT follow the rescue. One passenger recognizes the maker’s mark on Ratchet’s necklace clasp and points to the Cache. It connects the workshops without yet revealing the full history of the ancestral gift.

### Crystal Cache interlude — Ask Before You Take

A quiet change of pace after the aerial fight. Kerf detects footsteps through the floor. Communication is tactile and visual, and the scene lets the cat be competent without a tutorial speech.

The Prism Prowler guards the quarry containing the raw marble for the second blade. It is clean. Remove “virus destroyed” wording from this encounter. Its aggression is a boundary, not a disease to cure. At first the cat mistakes the familiar guardian setup for another infection and tries the PURIFIER; it reveals no command binding. This failure forces her to learn the distinction.

**Fight:** Prowler mirrors the cat's approach and punishes repeated lunges. The objective is to disarm the test mechanism and hold a safe position, not drain the opponent into submission. The player can win a conventional spar or solve the protection circuit; both culminate in Kerf opening the quarry after the cat stops attacking and helps repair its supports. In the comic, the cat puts her sword down first, then repairs the Prowler's damaged footing while keeping out of its reach.

**Material and forging rule:** the caves contain naturally occurring hush marble nodules. The hero harvests a second raw nodule; Kerf shapes it into a second blade at her bench using her cutting skills and Ratchet’s method. Neither blade was an ancient sword divided in two. A later, separately found connector couples the two already made weapons. Show retrieval, shaping and grant as separate events.

**Cost:** Kerf fears reconnecting anything. The cat promises a local connection with a physical disconnect, not a return to the old network. Their disagreement remains relevant in the finale.

**Payoff:** permanent dual swords and hurricane; independent blade handling is taught in a safe exit room. The Prowler may follow at a distance by choice. A mark on Kerf’s oldest tool matches the necklace clasp and plants the Archive revelation about the ancient harvester’s gift.

### Chapter Three — The Furnace Must Stay Warm

The Foundry builds bodies and provides district heat. The Eye has tied manufacture and life-support into the same controls. “Shut it down” would stop the infected production line but also freeze powered-down refugees.

Patch-7 wants four gun emplacements quiet so he can reach a bypass. Make those locations part of one visible pressure-routing problem. A player sees what each disabled emplacement allows him to repair. The quest can still reuse its existing counters, but it needs distinct outcomes in the room.

**Action:** FURNACE CHOIR, the dragon, regulates heat. Its forced roar opens the wrong valves. The dual blades give HZD-99 control of two nearby bindings during pauses; hurricane clears a closing wave of molten constructs but spends charge she may need for recovery. COOLANT buys a short safe route. The player learns these applications before the full boss combination.

**Reversal:** the dragon's apparent attack on a refuge wall is an attempt to open an emergency heat duct. Patch notices the warm airflow first. The heroes must preserve part of its action while interrupting the command that turns it lethal. Do not repeat the lion's exact cable solution.

**Character choice:** HZD-99 cannot pursue the fleeing KILN-MOTH and hold the emergency valve at once. She chooses the valve and trusts Patch to stop the construct. In the game either approach can work if the player has prepared the appropriate route; an unprepared route loops back through a recoverable task, not a campaign-ending loss.

**Payoff:** FURNACE CHOIR and Foundry Sage restored, stable heat, FORGE and existing post-guardian rewards. Beyond the repaired wall lies the connector, a distinct found object with a distinct acquisition event. Patch fits it; dual mode remains available. The joined returning throw is learned after installation, not improvised in the preceding boss fight.

**Ending image:** a new body leaves the line, opens its eyes, and chooses to turn away from the assigned work station. One small choice gives the restoration meaning.

### Chapter Four — What We Chose to Forget

The Frozen Archives preserve memory in a medium the live signal struggles to cross. The Eye sends LATTICE to destroy physical records that cannot be remotely rewritten. The kingdom becomes a race between retrieval and erasure, with calm rooms for reading.

The Nine-Lives Sage has attempted this rescue before. Their usefulness is a route annotated with mistakes, not a speech that makes the player afraid to experiment. The cold index preserves testimony about the planned return of local authority, the Eye’s intercepted routing changes, and the old workshop records.

**Major revelation:** the Eye hijacked the Song when the kingdoms planned to retire its central authority. HZD-99’s retirement record makes the parallel personal: both were considered obsolete. A second, older record shows a cave harvester gifting the marble to Ratchet’s ancestor after being rescued. That kindness, not a planned defense weapon, enabled the cure. Mother has been deliberately creating gaps in the corrupted song so her listeners can resist.

Seed these facts with the retirement ticket, routing forms, the matching maker’s mark and missing musical notes. Preserve Mother’s innocence. Neither she nor the sages authorized violent obedience. They must restore the route the Eye stole and make future local control physically enforceable.

**Action:** GLACIERE's lance, shards and freezing patterns guard the protected records. The cat's returning joined blade can cut an exposed command line behind cover and still return to her hand; the outward and return paths matter. FORGE creates limited safe footing, not immunity to the arena. The player must alternate movement and control rather than remain still spamming throws.

**Reversal:** destroying the central archive would erase the Eye's local access but also erase the evidence and the instructions for isolating Mother. The cat entrusts one blade/core to the Sage temporarily for a local circuit while fighting in an already learned single mode. This is a scripted equipment handoff with a guaranteed return, never permanent loss of earned ownership.

**Payoff:** GLACIERE and the Archive Sage freed, Kernel Key/HALT retained in the established progression, physical records preserved. The route to the Nest opens. The ancestral workshop record survives; Ratchet receives it through a deliberately isolated text channel and commits to reopening the old quarry route.

### Chapter Five — A Song That Can Be Refused

The Nest is the network grown around Mother's captivity. Lumen knows which passages remain safe. Her light is communication and fear made visible, not a generic collectible guide. Recovering her beacon lens lets her project a route for other rescuers as well as the protagonist.

The Eye's final construct, THE LENS, watches the approach. HZD-99 uses the Cache's touch signals and offline paths to get allies past it. Destroying the Lens deprives the Eye of local vision; he must physically confront the breach through a control frame inside the Null Core complex.

**Mother encounter:** preserve the imposing MOTHER-V form as the hijacked broadcast body. The task is to isolate its command amplifiers while protecting its core. Its dangerous patterns are commands imposed through her body, with explicit moments when she creates a pause. Use the established waveform distinction so the player can learn which opening comes from her. Avoid making falsified HUD data the only source of essential survival information.

**Mother's decision:** through Lumen's restored beacon and the isolated route, the cat can ask rather than assume. Mother chooses silence while the physical couplings are removed. She does not need to be killed for the lights to return.

**Eye confrontation:** the Eye offers HZD-99 the vacant authority. She could order every robot to become safe. The tempting offer is concrete: stop all enemies, repair the world's schedules, and end every failed rescue. He uses her retirement wound: “They put you away when they no longer needed you. Make them need you forever.” Her earlier habit of carrying every task gives that offer credibility, but her friendships have taught her another way.

She refuses by action. Instead of taking the command console, she returns the local control cores to the representatives of the kingdoms. Kerf's physical disconnects and the sages' independent routes keep any one relay from speaking as everybody. The allies do not sing one compulsory note; each answers voluntarily.

**Final fight:** the Eye's frame tries to reconnect three exposed control arms. Single sword precisely severs one narrow binding; dual hurricane holds back a short convergence of constructs; joined throw reaches the last coupling while the cat moves to shelter. These are optional tactical applications of earned modes, not a mandatory rapid menu sequence. All three are viable throughout; arena geometry creates reasons to prefer them.

HZD-99 cuts the command machinery out of the Eye's frame. He remains an isolated, powerless robot. The communities can decide how to hold him accountable; he is not rewarded with instant friendship or secretly controlling the sequel.

**Planet-wide cure:** the allies have already harvested marble and built resonance couplers during the final approach. With the Eye isolated and Mother cleansed, the sages carry the established cleansing resonance through locally controlled relays. This frees robots beyond the hero’s physical reach; show the preparation before the payoff.

**Resolution:** Mother speaks one short sentence in her own voice: “May I?” The assembled machines can answer. Some listen; some continue their work; Kerf feels the rhythm through the floor. The first voluntary song begins. HZD-99 returns to a workshop with a lamp somebody else has already repaired.

Ratchet reopens the old quarry route with rescued workers. NULLFANG rests where travelers can shelter. Mono records corrections openly. Lumen dims comfortably beside company. The final image is HZD-99 choosing to nap in an open workshop: her new cradle is a place to rest, not forced retirement. Ratchet’s note reads, “REST WHEN YOU WANT. WORK WHEN YOU’RE READY.”

## Game adaptation: shared facts, different presentation

The comic follows one emotionally coherent route. The game lets the player discover, attempt, fail, recover and choose approaches within the same causal facts. Do not require panel-for-panel duplication. A long comic duel might be a five-minute playable encounter; a long optional platforming route might be one comic transition.

### Proposed Chapter One state flow

`peaceful morning / sleep / hijack prologue → wake → safe movement/jump → active hazard and scrap → find Ratchet’s own battery → restore Ratchet → buy volt pack → practice repair/Burst → restore Servo → guardian encounter → marble cave → raw nodule → return/shape/forge → first local cure → first sage → coordinated relay fight → free NULLFANG → isolate district → receive guardian upgrades → Conduits`

The first-sage route must require the forged sword but not NULLFANG's reward. The cave and return route require neither FireDash nor double-jump. Before buying the pack, charge can accumulate but neither heal nor Burst is available. The opening salvage guarantees the pack price and cannot be permanently spent on another item during this lesson.

### Make rescue mechanically honest

Add explicit actor categories: infected person, clean hostile, empty construct. Do not infer them from sprite color alone. Mark bindings and constructs with distinct shapes and behavior so the player can identify the appropriate action.

For infected common enemies after the first forge: stagger → exposed binding → short cleansing interaction. Claw/weapon hits still provide active combat; cleansing is the completion action. Interruptions reset the opening rather than permanently killing the person. Give optional targeting assistance without requiring a tiny touch-screen precision hit.

Before the forge, enemies can be disabled and left recoverable; industrial salvage and defeated empty constructs supply spending resources. If implementation continues to destroy infected bodies, the script must acknowledge that cost. Do not merely rename deaths “rescues.”

Reward successful cures with charge, recovered external scrap, route help or a distinct service. Keep the economic value competitive with destructive encounters. Do not require the player to farm named rescued people. Enemy replay instances must be clearly training echoes or new threats, not the same person inexplicably reinfected after every bench rest.

Keep the current tame-only guardian policy for the first revised release. A lethal campaign would require different relationships, services, endings and balance; an extra damage bonus alone is not sufficient consequence. It is a separate future scope, not a switch to turn back on for the appearance of interactivity.

### Smooth play and retry

- One current control prompt at a time, with the actual remapped keyboard, controller or touch binding. Comic characters never name those bindings.
- Dialogue stays short at the critical path. Optional conversations and environmental records carry depth. Skipping a film never skips an objective, reward, tutorial state or equipment grant.
- Save quest stage, equipment ownership, equipped mode, rescued NPC states, local relay state and earned rewards separately. Grants are idempotent; room re-entry cannot grant a second sword or duplicate a guardian reward.
- Checkpoint before major multi-phase encounters. On death, restore a valid attempt state and recover thrown equipment. Never spend a unique story cell twice on retry.
- Preserve the Husk loop as recoverable resources and a readable return route. Default difficulty does not permanently erase named relationships or corrupt the story record because the player died. The separately selected Nine Lives mode retains its disclosed stakes.
- Show short charge opportunities before the quarry and each required charged-action lesson. The player cannot spend all charge healing and become unable to quarry; a safe recharge option remains accessible.
- Put the earned dash lesson after NULLFANG, the earned double-jump lesson after TALONHOST, hurricane after the second sword, returning throw after the connector. No fight solution depends on an unearned move.
- Ordinary combat can remain brisk. Reserve longer cleansing sequences for first demonstrations and major guardians; repeat cures should take seconds, not trigger the same film every time.
- Optional rematch mode reuses arenas with declared simulation/echo framing. It can remix telegraphs, enemy positions and loadouts without undoing campaign rescues.

## Reader participation without breaking the story

The reader should predict, notice, worry and reinterpret. In Chapter One they can spot the safe cave route, recognize NULLFANG's protective motion, anticipate the gap in a pulse and see the opened CHIME vent before the cat acts. Those are forms of engagement available in the printed book.

For the web reader, add optional extras after a scene or episode: inspect the arena route, compare a clue with its later payoff, play a short licensed/original clean and corrupted musical motif, or open a character's recovered record. Provide text/shape equivalents for sound clues and no autoplay audio.

An optional route-prediction question can reveal different explanations, but it does not pretend to rewrite the published chapter. Persistent branching should exist only when authored consequences exist. The first web edition needs no account, vote tally or analytics collection to feel participatory.

Invite play by naming the action the reader just wanted to attempt: reach the relay, read the lion's feint, or choose a route through the transit fight. Link to that encounter only when it is actually implemented. Keep the main dramatic ending intact and put the invitation after a visual break.

## Production and acceptance

### Writing gates

For each scene, record: who wants what; what prevents it; what choice they make; what changes; what the next scene now requires. A scene that only reports a reward should be combined, cut or given a consequence.

For each twist, record its earlier visible clues and what it changes afterward. A revelation that simply adds a new name is not enough. The Eye's defeat and Mother's survival must be settled by the main story, not postponed into advertising for a sequel.

### Art gates

Use the editorial review's identity contract. Start with a limited proof sequence before spending generation effort on an entire book. The full comic does not become approved because its prompts mention references. Record the actual output review independently from text accuracy and publication status.

Panel script, reference IDs, equipment state, VFX family, injuries and previous/next framing belong in a production manifest. Any replacement panel must be checked against its neighbors. Old images can remain in an archive but cannot be selected by current page manifests or used as silent fallbacks.
