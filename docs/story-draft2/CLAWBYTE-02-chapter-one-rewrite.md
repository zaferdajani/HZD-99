# CLAWBYTE — Chapter One: The Machine That Said No

Complete replacement comic script, draft 2. Forty story pages plus a cover; full color, top-to-bottom reading. Each page is also a discrete vertical-scroll beat. The physical-page numbers below are production identifiers, not a requirement to force print gutters into a webtoon.

This is proposed new writing, not a transcript of the current game. The owner’s clarified foundation is fixed: a peaceful robot planet beginning each day with Mother’s song; an older hero deliberately put to sleep; Ratchet protected by an inherited marble before removing his own battery; retrieval of that battery; raw cave marble brought home and shaped into the first cleansing sword. Read CLAWBYTE-00-story-foundation.md for the complete revised canon proposal and survivor histories. This script also moves the first sage before the guardian climax and makes CHIME an active relay; those changes require implementation before the comic can depict the shipped game.

HZD-99 remains mostly wordless. Her decisions, visor expression, ears, posture and short sounds carry her performance. No thought balloons explain what the drawing already shows. All unspecified panels are silent. Dialogue below is final-draft candidate text; bracketed staging is never lettered into the comic.

## Cover

HZD-99, tiny beneath NULLFANG's raised paw, reaches toward a damaged cable rather than raising a sword against his throat. Her red scarf catches a narrow white light from Ratchet’s distant workshop; her paws are empty. In the distance, one hanging chime rings over the scrap fields. Keep the lion's expression dangerous and conflicted, not friendly. Title: **CLAWBYTE**. Chapter title: **The Machine That Said No**. This is spoiler-light cover art. She has no sword or sword-shaped object on the cover; the first weapon reveal belongs to the forge.

## Page 1 — A planet answers

1. Wide sunrise over a robot planet: green scrap terraces, shining transit canals, warm Foundry towers, cold Archive peaks. The kingdoms occupy one connected world, not unrelated level backdrops.
2. Mother’s familiar radial form, using the current approved design, begins the morning song. Letter one short lyric: **“A light for you. A light for me.”**
3. Across three linked panels, the kingdoms answer in different rhythms. Use the approved crawler, hopper, guard, flier and small machine-folk sheets in everyday work.
4. A crawler props up a crooked stall. A shield guard holds a door for tiny workers carrying an oversized battery. There is individuality and gentle disagreement inside the harmony.
5. Two workers begin the same task, notice each other, laugh in their own mechanical way and divide it. Nobody is being ordered to agree.

## Page 2 — Places for everyone

1. NULLFANG lets small maintenance units polish his mane. One polisher tickles a joint; his mighty sneeze sends a rag flying.
2. TALONHOST catches the rag before it lands in a transit canal. Below, Servo turns his winding drum in time with the song.
3. FURNACE CHOIR carefully lights a furnace while GLACIERE cools a reservoir. Keep their established designs; these are the same bodies the reader will later see infected.
4. In a lower tunnel, Lumen guides a worker home. Elsewhere, Kerf follows the morning rhythm by touch and pulsing lamps. Mono and Patch-7 appear in their recognizable workplaces.
5. A small older HZD-99 squeezes behind a workshop cabinet to reconnect a wire newer units cannot reach. A worker from the approved background roster acknowledges her with a warm nod as the lamp comes on. This is her former service workshop, distinct from Ratchet's shop.
6. A newer maintenance group arrives beside a service ticket: **LEGACY UNITS — SLEEP STORAGE.** HZD-99 looks from the lit lamp to the ticket.

## Page 3 — Until we need you

1. A workshop keeper gently seats HZD-99 in the cradle. Use a background worker from the approved roster, not a new unexplained major character.
   - KEEPER: “Just until an old system needs you again.”
2. Her service ticket reads **RETAIN FOR LEGACY MAINTENANCE**. The next-wake-date field is blank.
3. The group leaves to answer Mother’s next morning note. HZD-99 reaches after them; the cradle closes its receiver contact first, then her eyes go dark.
4. Far below, a routing display lists a forthcoming transfer: **LOCAL CONTROL / CENTRAL AUTHORITY RETIRED.** The Eye’s mechanical hand stops above it.
5. He overlays a repeating command on Mother’s waveform.
   - EYE: “They will still need me.”
6. The same lyric from page 1 begins across the planet. Beneath it appears a smaller, intrusive mark: **OBEY.**

## Page 4 — The morning breaks

1. Repeat the shield guard’s open-door composition. It slams the door on the workers it was helping. Red command marks force its arm down.
2. The crawler drives its tool toward the stall keeper. NULLFANG turns violently toward a fleeing maintenance unit. Show force and peril without indulging in gore.
3. Mother strains against the overlay. One note falls silent. In the brief gap, a worker pulls another out of the guard’s path.
4. Ratchet’s hand involuntarily reaches for a weapon, then stops as the round marble on his necklace shines. His eyes clear. He understands that he is still himself while the street is not.
5. Through his workshop window: one shutter after another closes. Inside, he writes a note, slides a battery drawer into position and grips the mechanical release beside his chair. Do not show him simultaneously functioning normally with his battery already removed.
6. Time passes in the same composition: Ratchet dark, the note hanging from his chest, the marble still against him. Cut to HZD-99’s forgotten cradle, untouched by the broadcast.

The next page’s reserve wake is a later power-diversion event. Its cause is visible in the cradle gauge; the Eye did not intentionally create or summon the hero.

## Page 5 — The unfinished job

1. Tight on a dark maintenance cradle. A mechanical indicator reads **LEGACY MAINTENANCE / RESERVE WAKE**. The main supply drops to zero; an independent spring contact closes. Dust has settled across the switch.
2. An overhead lamp tries to illuminate. Click. Darkness. Click. Darkness.
3. Two cyan eyes come on. A cable drops from the little cat's chassis. No sword, pack or weapon fittings beyond her established body.
4. HZD-99 sits up, watches the lamp fail again, and reaches toward it.
5. Her paw cannot reach. She extends one claw an absurd extra fraction. Still short.

The first question is small and legible: what will she do?

## Page 6 — A useful cat

1. She drags a battered parts box beneath the lamp.
2. From the box, she reconnects a loose contact. The lamp lights.
3. The light reveals a workshop full of dust and abandoned stations. Her pleased eyes narrow, then change. Nobody is there to notice.
4. She gives a short questioning chirp toward the doorway. No answer.
5. Outside, another lamp repeats the same broken pulse. She takes a small empty tool pouch from the cradle and follows the light.

She carries no spare Power Cell. Ratchet’s own hidden battery must be found in his workshop.

## Page 7 — Everybody is still working

1. Wide: the gate opens onto the Scrap Meadows. Cranes sort the same heaps; a conveyor runs into its own blockage. This is an industrial kingdom with a recognizable daily purpose.
2. A street repair unit paints a line against a closed wall. Its tool returns and repeats. Its eye burns red with a broken waveform.
3. HZD-99 tries to catch its attention. It turns its head a fraction toward her chirp, then lunges with its pointed marking tool.
4. She ducks behind a barrier. A signal mark flickers across a nearby loudspeaker and forces the unit back to its wall. Work and violence are both imposed; the cat cannot mistake it for a harmless sleepy neighbor.
5. A forge sign lights briefly down a side street: **RATCHET — REPAIRS**. Beneath it, an unattended machine strains against a jammed cable.

No apocalypse lecture. The reader sees useful work turned into compulsion.

## Page 8 — The first attack

1. HZD-99 crawls beneath the straining winch to clear the jam. Establish cable, scrap chute and safe recess in the same wide panel.
2. The winch pivots at the command pulse. Its moving arm traps her scarf against the chute.
3. She claws the scarf free, losing a tiny corner of cloth. One joint in her forearm sparks from the impact.
4. She ducks into the established recess. The arm hits the chute. The jam breaks; loose industrial scrap spills out.
5. Instead of striking the machine's face, she cuts its exposed drive belt. It stops. Its red eye remains lit.
6. She looks from the disabled unit to her damaged arm. Then to Ratchet's sign.

She has survived and bought time; she has not cured it. The recovered scrap comes from the chute, not the body's destruction.

## Page 9 — Someone at the counter

1. Inside the shop, a huge copper figure sits dark. His hand is extended toward an absent tool. Small metal pieces are sorted into meticulous trays.
2. HZD-99 taps his counter. Then his knee. No response.
3. A card on his chest: **IF THE SONG IS SAFE, PUT MY CELL BACK. — R.** Below, in smaller writing: **IF IT ISN'T, PLEASE BE CAREFUL.**
4. The back of the note carries a simple service diagram: a hand, a pulley and a drawer beneath the chair. She follows its cable and lifts the jammed counterweight.
5. The drawer opens. Inside is Ratchet’s own battery, its stamped serial matching the empty socket in his chest. She looks toward the open back door, then back to him.
6. She puts his battery back into its socket.

The first workshop puzzle is readable in the art and safely repeatable in play. She chooses to wake a stranger rather than leave through the back exit. She does not sacrifice or remove her own battery.

## Page 10 — Ratchet

1. Ratchet wakes violently and grabs her wrist. His other hand clamps over the rounded marble in its old necklace clasp.
   - RATCHET: “Is it singing?”
2. She freezes. He sees her cyan eyes and releases her.
   - RATCHET: “Sorry. Old habit. New terror.”
3. He leans toward the door, listens, and lowers the shutter.
   - RATCHET: “How long?”
4. She points to the dust outline where his hand rested.
5. Ratchet looks at it. A long, quiet beat.
   - RATCHET: “Ah.”
6. He notices the torn scarf and sparking wrist, and instinctively reaches for the missing tool again. She puts a nearby spanner into his hand.

Their first connection is doing something for one another.

## Page 11 — The wrong note

1. A faint note slips through the shutter. Its visual waveform forks into a repeated command.
2. HZD-99's paw begins to turn toward the sound against her effort. A small red intrusion appears at the edge of the visor, never replacing its design.
3. Ratchet presses the necklace’s marble near her receiver and pulls her behind the grounded workbench. The intrusion breaks. He closes the shop’s receiver shutter, then disconnects the automatic broadcast link in her service panel, leaving ordinary hearing intact.
   - RATCHET: “Don't answer.”
4. HZD-99 looks at her paw, shaken.
   - RATCHET: “Mother used to sing us home.”
5. Close on the pale round marble. Its old metal clasp bears a tiny maker’s mark; the stone is intact.
   - RATCHET: “Someone put an order inside her voice.”
6. The cat points to his empty socket cover, then the stone.
   - RATCHET: “This kept my mind mine. I took out my battery before I learned its limits.”

She is not immune. Her disconnected sleep state saved her during the original broadcast. The necklace clears the present intrusion; receiver isolation gives her a limited safe route to the caves. Ratchet does not promise immunity to direct relay contact.

## Page 12 — Twelve pieces

1. Ratchet sets out the compact volt pack. HZD-99 reaches. He puts a tiny invoice in front of it.
   - RATCHET: “Twelve.”
2. She looks at him. Looks at his battery, which she has just reinstalled. Looks at him again.
   - RATCHET: “Waking fee is waived.”
3. She empties the chute scrap from her pouch. He counts, making a show of business because he is frightened.
4. Ratchet installs the pack in its approved mounting position.
   - RATCHET: “You collect charge. This lets you use it.”
5. He guides her paw toward the damaged wrist. A controlled repair effect closes the split; the gauge drains visibly.
   - RATCHET: “Mend...”
6. She tests a brief release into a metal target. It knocks a tray over. Ratchet catches it against his belly.
   - RATCHET: “...or make a point.”

Purchase comes before both healing and Burst. Keep the tray gag small; the power is real, but she is learning.

## Page 13 — A repair he cannot make

1. Ratchet spreads a physical sketch: workshop, winding house, cave and the chamber beneath the lion's enclosure. Four landmarks, one visible route.
   - RATCHET: “The Meadow Sage is still down there. If anyone can shut that signal out, it's them.”
2. He touches the necklace.
   - RATCHET: “A cave harvester gave this to my ancestor. It came down with the workshop.”
3. HZD-99 studies the round stone, then the drawn cave on his map. Ratchet understands.
   - RATCHET: “Bring me a larger marble. I can shape it into a sword that clears the virus.”
4. She offers to take his necklace now. His hand covers it too quickly.
   - RATCHET: “Not this one.”
5. Embarrassed, he gives her the cave sketch and a separate spare cell from his locked chest. While they talk, the workshop cradle tops up her newly installed pack; its indicator visibly fills.
   - RATCHET: “Servo knows the gantries. If he's still there.”
6. As she goes:
   - RATCHET: “Come back for the invoice.”

The necklace is an inherited gift, not a daughter-made crystal or a fragment of a pre-existing weapon. His fear and its small size explain why he sends her for more raw material rather than dismantling his only protection.

## Page 14 — The winding house

1. HZD-99 reaches Servo's dark winding station. The broken bridge beyond is visible behind him.
2. She inserts the spare cell. His drum shudders into motion.
   - SERVO: “Cargo weight?”
3. She stands on the scale. Its pointer barely moves.
   - SERVO: “Is the cargo coming later?”
4. She points at herself, firmly.
5. He notices her torn scarf, and then the silent landscape.
   - SERVO: “I shut the signal out. Lowered the bridge. That used the last of me.”
6. He starts raising the bridge, but a coil slips free from the upper wheel.
   - SERVO: “Top pulley. Put that back and I can get you across.”

## Page 15 — A guardian's work

1. HZD-99 climbs the framework, carrying the loose coil. No dash or double-jump; she uses claws, ladders and ordinary jumps.
2. NULLFANG lands on the upper gantry. Establish his full shape against her tiny body.
3. She spots an old scratched emblem on his shoulder: the same bridge-and-shelter mark on Servo's machinery.
4. The lion raises a paw. HZD-99 shelters behind a hanging counterweight.
5. The paw smashes the weight aside and sends her onto a lower platform. Her forearm is hurt again; not the scarf side changing at random.
6. NULLFANG follows. The platform buckles beneath them.

## Page 16 — The hesitation

1. Wide: the cat hangs from the platform edge. NULLFANG's huge forepaw is planted between her and a falling support beam.
2. His red signal brightens. He tries to lift the paw toward her.
3. His own rear claw hooks a retaining cable instead. The beam stops falling. Give the reader a clean close-up of that deliberate action.
4. HZD-99 scrambles under it. For one instant, the lion looks toward her without moving.
5. A command pulse from the distant CHIME makes him wrench backward. The retaining cable slices a recognizable groove into his armor.
6. Servo lowers a service lift beneath HZD-99.
   - SERVO: “He used to catch the falling ones.”

The hero survives because a protector is still resisting. The meaning of that resistance becomes the chapter's mystery.

## Page 17 — Under the noise

1. HZD-99 reaches the cave mouth on the sketch, under the repaired gantry.
2. The Mother's distorted waveform weakens with each step into rock.
3. At a fork, one route has polished rail, speaker wire and regularly spaced lights; the other has hand-cut steps and irregular touch marks.
4. Close on three scratched symbols: an ear crossed out, a hand, an open doorway.
5. HZD-99 touches the marks, then takes the hand-cut route.
6. Behind her, the lit rail continues toward a sealed dead end bearing the command waveform.

The reader can infer the safe route before she takes it. Do not insert an unseen riddle and announce its solution.

## Page 18 — People who listened differently

1. A refuge: low tables, tactile route maps, insulated lamps and an empty chair repaired for an unusual body shape. Signs of a community, not an exposition terminal.
2. HZD-99 repeats a little chirp. Nothing answers; she notices the lamps are triggered by touch.
3. Pressing a contact lights the path toward a seam of round marble nodules in ordinary host rock.
4. One brief engraved message: **WE ARE BELOW. KNOCK.**
5. She knocks on a door. A pause.
6. Only the last loose bolt in the door rattles. She catches it, screws it back in, and continues.

Do not reveal Kerf here. Preserve the later discovery of a living inhabitant.

## Page 19 — One charge

1. A rounded pale marble nodule is embedded in a dark seam. Her ordinary claws scrape the surrounding host rock but cannot loosen it. The material must remain visibly stone-shaped, with no blade, handle or sword silhouette.
2. She lifts the injured arm; its joint still shakes from NULLFANG's blow. Her pack shows enough stored charge for one meaningful expenditure.
3. Through a crack in the wall, the signal climbs. Two quarry crawlers turn toward the opening. She is running out of quiet time, not undergoing an unexplained universal infection timer.
4. She places the good paw over the injured arm to begin mending, then stops.
5. She braces the damaged wrist with a strip torn from the already damaged scarf corner.
6. The Burst gathers. Her silhouette and visor remain unchanged inside the light.

## Page 20 — The marble in her paws

1. Burst breaks the host rock around the nodule. One whole raw marble comes free. The rest of the seam remains intact.
2. With the gauge now low, HZD-99 catches the rounded stone in both paws. It is bulky, awkward, and emphatically not a weapon.
3. Its light reveals the command filaments reaching toward the crawlers' receiver housings. This is a diagnostic perception, not a second set of monster bodies.
4. She notices one crawler shielding a small tool cart even while its drill points at her.
5. Near the marble, the incoming filaments falter. The crawlers remain infected beyond its small protective field. She cannot safely bring an uncontrolled lump to each moving receiver; a shaped, directed cleansing edge will let her act in combat.
6. She pulls the tactile-route door closed and uses the known service path out. The light inside stays on.

## Page 21 — The return

1. Ratchet is waiting in the doorway pretending to oil a hinge that no longer squeaks.
   - RATCHET: “That took a while.”
2. HZD-99 sets the raw marble down. It rolls across the bench; Ratchet catches it in a cup. Then, with some difficulty, she offers the injured arm.
3. He sees the scarf splint and understands what she spent the charge on.
   - RATCHET: “You came back in installments.”
4. He starts repairing the joint with ordinary workshop tools. She points to the lion mark on the map, then mimes the caught beam.
5. Ratchet's expression changes.
   - RATCHET: “He held it?”
6. She nods.
   - RATCHET: “Then he's still in there.”

This is the midpoint discovery. The combat goal changes from surviving a monster to reaching a prisoner.

## Page 22 — An edge, not an answer

1. Ratchet places the raw rounded marble in a shaping jig. HZD-99 steadies its clamp with her repaired arm. Show the intact nodule before any blade exists.

3. He adds a handle and an energy fitting. An unused standard coupling socket remains visible.
   - RATCHET: “I leave room for improvements.”
4. She points at the empty fitting.
   - RATCHET: “No. You cannot have all of them today.”
5. He gives her the finished PURIFIER.
   - RATCHET: “Cut what is holding them. Not what is left of them.”

## Page 23 — The first freed machine

1. The disabled winch from page 8 is where she left it. The broken drive belt remains on the ground.
2. With the blade nearby, the receiver's imposed command becomes visible. She studies it rather than attacking blindly.
3. In the quiet interval between distant pulses, she makes one careful cut across the exposed command filament, clear of its memory housing. This small receiver has no dedicated renewing relay like the guardian's.
4. Its eye clears. The arm moves, very slowly, toward the scrap chute.
5. HZD-99 prepares to duck. The winch pushes her dropped pouch back to her instead.
6. It turns its tool toward its own broken belt. She kneels to help reconnect it.

No instant friendship speech or extra reward popup. One earlier action now has a different meaning. Ordinary-machine rescue is a proposed new mechanic, not a claim about the current game.

## Page 24 — The keeper below

## Page 25 — Wait for the space
