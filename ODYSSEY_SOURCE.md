# Homer's Odyssey — Source Research & Game Mapping

Research base: the public-domain prose translation by **Samuel Butler** (Project
Gutenberg eBook #1727, "public domain in the USA", verified 2026-07-19 at
https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1727) with the **Butcher & Lang** translation
(eBook #1728) as a cross-reference. All game text in this repository is
**original writing** based on the ancient myth itself — nothing is copied from
any modern adaptation, film, or game.

## 1. The story in brief

The Odyssey (composed ~8th century BC, attributed to Homer) is the story of
**Odysseus**, king of the island of **Ithaca**, called *polymetis* — "the man of
many wiles." After ten years fighting at Troy he spends ten more trying to sail
home, wrecked and delayed by the wrath of the sea-god **Poseidon**, whose son he
blinded. While he wanders, his wife **Penelope** holds off more than a hundred
arrogant **suitors** who occupy his palace, devour his herds, and press her to
remarry; she stalls them by weaving a funeral shroud by day and secretly
unraveling it by night. His son **Telemachus** searches for news of him, guided
by the goddess **Athena**, Odysseus' constant protector, who often walks among
mortals in disguise (as the old family friend "Mentor"). In the end Odysseus
returns alone, disguised as a beggar, is recognized first by his old dog Argos
and his nurse Eurycleia, wins the contest of stringing his own great bow, kills
the suitors with it, proves himself to Penelope through the secret of their
marriage bed (carved from a living olive tree), and is reunited with his family.

## 2. The major episodes (Books I–XXIV)

| Books | Episode |
|---|---|
| I–IV | **The Telemachy**: the gods in council; Athena visits Ithaca; Telemachus confronts the suitors and sails to Pylos (Nestor) and Sparta (Menelaus & Helen) for news. |
| V | **Calypso**: the nymph has kept Odysseus seven years on Ogygia; Hermes carries Zeus' order to release him; Poseidon wrecks his raft; he swims ashore in Scheria. |
| VI–VIII | **The Phaeacians**: princess Nausicaa finds the castaway; King Alcinous hosts him; the bard's song of Troy makes him weep, and he reveals his name. |
| IX | **His tale begins — the Cicones, the Lotus-eaters** (whose flower makes men forget home), **and the Cyclops**: trapped in the cave of **Polyphemus**, the one-eyed giant who eats his men, Odysseus gets him drunk, tells him his name is "**Noman**" (*Outis*), blinds him with a heated olive stake, and escapes clinging beneath the bellies of the giant's rams. When the other Cyclopes ask who hurt him, Polyphemus cries "Noman is killing me!" — and they leave. Odysseus' parting boast reveals his true name, and Polyphemus prays to his father Poseidon for revenge. |
| X | **Aeolus, the Laestrygonians, Circe**: Aeolus gifts a leather bag holding the storm-winds; in sight of Ithaca the crew opens it and the fleet is blown back. Giant Laestrygonians destroy eleven of the twelve ships. On Aeaea the enchantress **Circe** turns half the crew (led there by the wary **Eurylochus**, who alone hangs back and escapes) into swine; **Hermes** gives Odysseus the herb **moly** — "black at the root, with a milk-white flower" — which defeats her magic. Circe restores the crew and hosts them for a year, then directs Odysseus to the land of the dead. |
| XI | **The Nekyia — the visit to the dead**: at the edge of the world Odysseus pours blood offerings and speaks with the shades: the prophet **Tiresias** (who foretells his hard homecoming and warns him not to harm the Cattle of the Sun), his mother Anticleia, Agamemnon, Achilles ("better a poor serf alive than king of all the dead"), Ajax who turns away in silence, and the great sinners — Tantalus, Sisyphus. **Elpenor**, his youngest crewman, who fell drunk from Circe's roof, begs for burial. |
| XII | **The Sirens, Scylla and Charybdis, the Cattle of the Sun**: warned by Circe, Odysseus stops his crew's ears with **wax** but has himself lashed to the mast so he alone hears the **Sirens'** song, which promises all knowledge. In the strait beyond, **Scylla** — six heads on prodigious necks, each mouth triple-rowed with teeth, barking from her cliff cave — snatches six men, while the whirlpool **Charybdis** sucks down the sea three times a day. On Thrinacia the starving crew slaughters the sacred **Cattle of the Sun**; Zeus destroys the ship; only Odysseus survives, riding the wreckage back through Charybdis, saving himself by clinging to the great fig tree above her maw. He drifts to Calypso's isle. |
| XIII–XVI | **Return to Ithaca**: the Phaeacians land him asleep on his own shore; Athena disguises him as an old beggar; he shelters with the loyal swineherd **Eumaeus** and reveals himself to Telemachus. |
| XVII–XX | **The beggar in his own hall**: he endures the suitors' abuse (led by the arrogant **Antinous**, who strikes him with a footstool); the old dog **Argos** knows him and dies; the nurse **Eurycleia** recognizes the boar-tusk **scar** on his thigh. |
| XXI | **The contest of the bow**: Penelope promises to wed whoever can string Odysseus' great bow and shoot through twelve axe-heads. No suitor can bend it. The beggar strings it "as easily as a bard strings a lyre" and sends the arrow through all twelve. |
| XXII | **The slaying of the suitors**: Odysseus casts off his rags and, with Telemachus, Eumaeus and Philoetius — and Athena at his side — kills Antinous first and then all the suitors. |
| XXIII–XXIV | **Reunion and peace**: Penelope tests him with the secret of the olive-tree bed; the shades of the suitors descend; Laertes embraces his son; Athena imposes peace on Ithaca. |

## 3. Characters & monsters (as used by this game)

**Mortals & gods**: Odysseus · Penelope · Telemachus · Athena (grey-eyed
guardian, mistress of disguise) · Hermes (messenger, giver of moly) ·
Poseidon (the antagonist wrath) · Zeus · Circe (enchantress of Aeaea) ·
Calypso · Aeolus (keeper of winds) · Tiresias (blind prophet, met as a shade) ·
Eurylochus (wary second-in-command) · Elpenor (youngest crewman, dies at
Circe's roof) · Eumaeus (loyal swineherd) · Eurycleia (old nurse) ·
Antinous & Eurymachus (leaders of the suitors) · Nausicaa & Alcinous
(Phaeacia) · Argos (the old dog).

**Monsters & perils**: Polyphemus the Cyclops · the Lotus-eaters · the
Laestrygonian giants · Circe's transforming magic · the shades of the dead ·
the Sirens · Scylla (six-headed cliff horror) · Charybdis (the whirlpool) ·
the storm-winds · the wrath of Poseidon.

## 4. How the game maps onto the epic

NOSTOS (Greek νόστος, "homecoming" — the epic's central idea) compresses the
voyage into six interconnected zones. Liberties taken are noted; everything
else follows the myth.

| Zone | Episode | Boss (room) |
|---|---|---|
| A — The Storm-Wrecked Shores | Books V/XII: cast ashore among wreckage; lotus meadows nearby | **Charybdis** has surged into the shallows to finish the castaway (myth-bend: she leaves her strait) |
| B — The Cyclops' Island | Book IX: caves, flocks, the giant's pastures | **Polyphemus**, hurling boulders (defeated → he grants nothing willingly; you loot **Hermes' winged-sandal stride** — see notes) |
| C — Circe's Isle | Book X: enchanted woods, transformed beasts | **Circe's Trial** — she tests the hero with blinking sorcery before yielding her counsel |
| D — The Underworld | Book XI: asphodel gloom, the shades, Tiresias | **Scylla** nests at the deep gate (myth-bend: her cave guards the passage back to the living world) |
| X — The Sirens' Strait (hidden) | Book XII: the song lures sailors **off the true course** — thus a secret zone found by the curious | **The Siren Queen**, the voice itself |
| E — Ithaca | Books XIII–XXII: the palace, the suitors | **Antinous, Champion of the Suitors** — final battle in the great hall |

Systems: god-**Blessings** (equippable crests with a socket budget) ·
**Treasures of the Voyage** (relics: the Ram's Fleece, a Moly Sprig, Scylla's
Sinew, the Siren's Plume, the Great Bow…) · **Oracle shrines** pose original
riddles that grant **Metis** (cunning) to unlock skills · **obols** (Charon's
coin) as currency, dropped in a purse on death · **hearth-fires** as rest/save
points · abilities: Wind of Aeolus (dash), Winged Sandals of Hermes (double
jump), Grip of the Fig Tree (wall-cling — Book XII), Aegis Pulse (Athena's
burst), Key of Persephone (opens the seal out of the Underworld).

The "Old Tongue" glyph script on votive tablets renders transliterated
Homeric Greek vocabulary (thalassa, nostos, xenos, kleos, metis…) — see
`js/lang.js`.

All dialogue, names of original constructs, riddles and Arabic translations
are written for this project. The Odyssey itself is ancient and public domain;
Butler's 1900 translation used for research is public domain in the USA.
